Channel strip

A high-pass, a low-pass, an EQ band, drive and level on the synth, the drums, the master and the tape tracks.

The channel strip shapes the synth and the drums before the tape, like the channel on a mixing desk. It sits before their effect, or after it if you like. There's one strip for the synth, one for the drums and a MASTER strip for everything you hear. They stay as they are when you change sounds or kits, and they are kept after a restart. They all start switched off, so nothing changes until you turn one on: hold [[Shift]] and turn green right.

Press [[mixer]], then [[T2]] for the EQ page, then hold [[Shift]] and press [[T2]]. The strip opens on the synth or the drums, whichever you were playing. Press [[Shift]] + [[T2]] again for the other strip, again for the tape strips, again for the MASTER strip, and once more to close it. You can also open a strip straight from an effect page: [[Shift]] + [[T2]] on the synth or drum effect page ([[T3]]) opens that strip, and on the mixer's master effect page it opens the MASTER strip. There a T key closes the strip and leaves you on the effect page. The keyboard keeps playing while it's open, so you can listen as you turn. Any mode key closes it too, and so does any T key except on the tape strips.

Most of the HPF and LPF travel sits between 200 Hz and 8 kHz, where you'll do most of your shaping. The first and last few steps move faster through the deep lows and the very top.

The line on the screen is what the strip does to the sound, from 20 Hz on the left to 20 kHz on the right. It moves as you turn and as an LFO moves the strip. Each control has a handle on the line in its encoder's colour: arrowheads for the HPF and LPF, a ring on a stem for the EQ band, a bracket over it for Q and a tick on the right edge for LEVEL. The small curve in the top right corner shows what DRIVE does to loud peaks. The values run along the bottom. Hold [[Shift]] to see LEVEL, ON or OFF (under PRE FX or POST FX), Q and DRIVE there instead. When an LFO moves the strip, a faint copy of the line shows where your knobs are. While the strip is off, the screen says OFF and the knobs still set the values, ready for when you switch it on.

The two thin bars on the right edge are meters: the left one shows what goes into the strip, the right one what comes out. They turn orange near the top. When a side hits full level and clips, the small mark above its bar lights orange for about a second. They catch every peak. They only move on the strip that's open, and on the synth or drum strip only while you play that instrument.

Push an encoder to put its control back to flat: blue turns the HPF off, green the LPF, white sets FREQ back to 1 kHz and orange GAIN to 0 dB. Hold [[Shift]] and push blue for LEVEL 0 dB, white for Q 0.7 or orange for no DRIVE. Hold [[Shift]] and push green to move the strip after the effect: the title says POST FX. Push again to put it back before the effect. The sound fades out and back in, so the move doesn't click. The idea is BarneyBrown's.

You can move the strip with an LFO. On a quad LFO page or the master LFO page, turn white past the other targets to reach HP, LP, FQ, GN, Q, DR and LV. On a pond LFO page it's orange, and the targets show their full names. The master LFO moves the synth, drum and master strips. The stock LFOs can't reach the strip. The strip saves the knob values you set, not where the LFO happens to be.

Every stage you leave at its start costs nothing. Each one you turn on uses a little CPU; with all five on, a heavy synth may get close to its limit.

A strip saved with 3.3 comes back as it was, before the effect. Its DRIVE knob sits a little lower than before, because the top half of DRIVE now goes further, but it sounds the same.

MASTER strip

The MASTER strip works on the whole stereo mix, after the master EQ. It sits before the master effect, or after it (and after the FX2 routing) when you hold [[Shift]] and push green. The controls are the same as on the other strips. It changes what you hear and what you resample, not what the tape records.

The title reads MASTER STRIP, or MASTER STRIP POST FX once you've moved it after the effect. The meters on the right follow whichever side is louder. The master LFO can move its controls just like on the synth and drum strips: pick HP, LP, FQ, GN, Q, DR or LV as the target.

The MASTER strip costs more CPU than the others, because it works on both sides of the stereo mix. Leave it off when you don't need it.

Tape strips

Each of the four tape tracks has a strip of its own. It sits after the tape and before the mixer's volume and pan, so

you can take the rumble off a mic take or tuck a bright track back into the mix after it's recorded.

On the strip page, press [[Shift]] + [[T2]] until the title says TRACK. It opens on the track that's selected on the tape.

Press [[T1]] to [[T4]] to pick another track. The four small squares at the top are the tracks: a filled square has its

strip on, and the box shows the one you're editing. The encoders work as on the synth and drum strips. Press

[[Shift]] + [[T2]] once more to close the page.

Two tape strips can be on at the same time. If you try to switch on a third, the OP-1 says 2 tape strips at most.

Hold [[Shift]] and push green to let the master LFO move this track's strip. LFO lights up next to the

squares. The master LFO reaches only the tracks you've set this way, and no other LFO reaches the tape strips.

Every tape keeps its own tape strips. Switch to another tape and its strips come back the way you left them.

A strip that's off, or on with every control flat, leaves the track exactly as it was. Tracks you send out over USB or

to FX2 carry the strip. A bounce doesn't: it copies the recorded tracks as they are.
