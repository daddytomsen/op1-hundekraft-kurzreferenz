ENTE

The pump, without the patch cables.

ENTE by op1r is a sidechain compressor: it can listen to something other than the sound it turns down. Put it on a synth, pick track 1 as the source, and the synth ducks every time the drums on track 1 hit. It can also listen to the input, or to a steady beat from the tempo and pump in time with no drums at all. You get anything from house-style pumping to a gentle breath under the drums. The rubber duck on screen gets squashed as ENTE turns the sound down, and it lies flat at 24 dB.

Load[[Shift]] + [[T3]] pick ente on a synth or drum slot

[[cap:knob-b]]
Threshold

The level where ducking starts, -48 to 0 dB

Push: Attack: how fast the ducking comes in, 0.1 to 50 ms. Around 5 to 30 ms pumps without a click

[[cap:knob-g]]
Ratio

How hard it ducks, 1:1 to 20:1, and all the way at the top

Push: Makeup: 0 to +24 dB, to bring the level back up

[[cap:knob-w]]
Release

How fast the sound comes back, 20 ms to 1 s

Push: High-pass on the source, off to 250 Hz, so a bass note doesn't set it off

[[cap:knob-o]]
Source

What ENTE listens to, one click per step. It wraps round

The sources:

  • SELF: the sound ENTE is on, like a normal compressor.
  • T1 to T4: one tape track, before its fader. TAPE: all four tracks. These work while the tape plays.
  • IN: the mic or line input.
  • INST: as the master effect, the synth or drums you play live. On a synth or the drums it's the same as SELF.
  • BEAT 1/1 to 1/16T: a short pulse at that note length on the OP-1's tempo, so you get the pumping without a sound to trigger it.

It starts with the threshold at -36 dB, the ratio at 8:1, a release of about 150 ms and the source on SELF. As a master effect ENTE turns left and right down together, so the stereo picture stays put.
