Amp

Your input is the instrument.

Amp turns the OP-1's live input into the instrument. The mic, line, radio or USB signal runs through a compressor, a tone control and an amp with a speaker cabinet. No key needs to be held. It is inspired by the OP-1 Field's live-input instrument.

To use it:

  1. Tick Amp in the workshop's Synths list, then install it (a full firmware or Review changes).
  2. On the OP-1, press [[synth]], hold [[Shift]] and press [[T1]]. Turn blue to amp and press [[T1]] to load it.
  3. Press the input key to switch monitoring on. Hold [[Shift]] and press the input key to choose mic, line, radio or USB.
  4. Feed audio into that source and raise blue Volume.
[[cap:knob-b]]
Volume

Output level; 0 mutes, 50 is unity, 100 adds about 6 dB

[[cap:knob-g]]
Comp

Compression; 0 is off, higher values tame peaks

[[cap:knob-w]]
Tone

Warm to bright; the middle is neutral and the screen shows 0 there

[[cap:knob-o]]
Drive

Saturation through the voice's amp and cabinet, with a gate that cuts hiss between notes; 0 is clean

Press blue to turn it into Voice: a box on the left lights up with a picture of the amp, and each click of blue steps to the next one: Clean, Crunch, Lead, Bass, Fuzz, High Gain or Metal. Each voice has its own tone stack, speaker cabinet and amount of drive, and the louder you play, the more it squashes, like a valve amp. Voice only changes the sound when Drive is above 0. Press blue again to get Volume back. Older sounds use Crunch, and sounds saved with a voice keep it.

High Gain is a modern high-gain amp. A boost cuts the lows before several gain stages in a row, so chords stay clear and single notes sustain. Its gate is tighter: stop a note and it goes quiet. Metal has even more gain, scooped mids, a tighter low end and a faster gate. Both are heavy from about a third of Drive up. The idea is u/Infamous_Call_6492's.

T1 shows the four values and the input as a moving line. T2 is a tuner for one sustained note. The needle leans left into red when the note is flat and right into blue when it is sharp, and stands in the green middle when it is in tune. Under the note name you see how many cents off it is. The input gain sits at the top right: turn blue on T2 to set it from -12 to +24 dB. It belongs to the input source, not the sound, so VOX and SNAP use the same setting. Use T3 to add an effect. T4 LFO modulation doesn't work with Amp. Keyboard notes, chord mode and sequencers don't gate or transpose the input. The settings save with the sound like any other.

Resampling the master (MASTER as the sampler source) is silent while Amp is the synth.
