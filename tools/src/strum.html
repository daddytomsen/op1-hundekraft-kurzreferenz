Strum

A real acoustic guitar that plays along.

Strum is a Martin HD28 acoustic guitar, recorded note by note. Every key plays the right chord for your key and scale, and Strum's right hand plays it in a pattern that follows the tempo. There are 28 patterns, from campfire strumming and funk to Travis picking, bossa nova and flamenco. Keep it in open chords or move up the neck to barre shapes; the neck on screen shows where every finger goes.

Load[[synth]] then [[Shift]] + [[T1]] pick strum

Every key strums straight away. Keep holding and the pattern goes on with the beat. Press another key while you hold one and it strums its chord at once, and the pattern carries on in time; it doesn't start over. Let go of the newest key and the chord goes back to the key you still hold on the next step. Let go of every key and the pattern stops, but the last strum rings out; the envelope's release sets how long. Push blue, green or white for page 2.

The top of the screen is the guitar's neck, high e on top as in a chord chart. Dots show where the chord is fingered (roots in blue), a green bar is a barre, and X and O at the nut are strings left out or played open. Strings shake while they ring. The red bar above the neck covers the frets your hand would span. Below the neck are four panels, one per encoder. The blue one shows the chord you're playing. The green one shows where that chord sits in the key, such as VIm in C. The white one shows the pattern as down and up strokes, thicker for accents, with a tick under the step that's playing. The red one shows the fret in big digits (00 is an open chord).

Key

[[cap:knob-g]]
Harmony

What each key plays: major, major 7, minor, minor 7, dorian, mixolydian, harmonic minor, sus, power or blues

Push: Human: strokes a little late and uneven, so it stops sounding like a machine

[[cap:knob-w]]
Pattern

28 strumming and picking patterns, listed below

Push: Spread: the time between strings in a strum, from a quick 10 ms to a lazy 45 ms

[[cap:knob-o]]
Neck

Where on the neck the chords sit: auto, open, or near the 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th or 12th fret

On page 2 a panel's tab shows the page-2 name and value, and the strokes in the white panel move with it: they swing late, wobble with Human and lean further the slower the strum.

Harmony decides the chord. In the scale harmonies each key plays the chord on its step of the scale: in C major, C plays C, D plays Dm, A plays Am. Major 7 and minor 7 play each step's seventh chord (Cmaj7, Dm7, G7, Am7 and so on). Sus plays sus2 or sus4, whichever fits the scale. Keys outside the scale play a major chord, the usual borrowed ones. Power and blues ignore the key: every key plays its own power chord or its own 7th.

Neck on auto picks the shapes by octave. Below middle C you get open chords where there is one, otherwise barre chords. Around middle C it plays barre chords further up the neck, and above that small four-string chords near the top. Open always plays the low shapes. The fret settings put every chord near that fret, whatever octave you play in. Turning Key, Harmony or Neck while you hold a key changes the chord on the next step.

The patterns come in three groups: strums, bass and strum, and picking. Picking patterns play single strings: the thumb takes the bass strings, the fingers the top ones.

A key pressed while a strum is still sounding cuts it short and strums its own chord, so a quick run of keys gives a string or two of each chord before the last one rings out.

Strum follows the OP-1's tempo and the tape. With the tape stopped it keeps the tempo you set.

There are twelve Strum presets: campfire, folk song, picker, lullaby, parlour waltz, slow ballad, palm mute, bright strum, let ring, bossa, celtic roll and flamenco.

The guitar samples are by Jeff Learman (Martin HD28, from the Discord SFZ GM Bank, released as CC0).
