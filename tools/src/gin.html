Gin

Gin is a drum synthesiser by SquareWave Studio, based on Eric Lewis's libpo32. Open the drum engine browser with [[Shift]] + [[T1]] and select GIN. DBOX and DRUM+ remain available.

Each kit has 24 drum sounds across the keyboard. Gin plays up to four synthesis voices at once. Repeating a sound chokes its previous hit; when all four voices are busy, the oldest voice is replaced. Eight factory kits appear in the Gin drum preset folder. These drum kits are separate from the downloadable synth preset pack.

On Play, strike a key to select and play its sound. Each sound has A and B settings and its own morph position. Push blue to choose which endpoint you edit. Moving morph towards A or B selects that endpoint; at the centre, the previous edit side stays selected. Changes affect new hits; ringing hits keep the settings they started with.

Press [[T1]] for Advanced. [[T2]]/[[T3]], or the arrows, move between Tone, Tone Envelope, Pitch Mod, Noise Filter, Noise Envelope, Mix And Level, EQ and Velocity. About follows the parameter pages. blue remains morph; the coloured labels show the other active controls. Press [[T1]] to return to Play.

On an Advanced parameter page, [[T4]] opens sound naming. blue or the arrows move the cursor, green chooses a character and orange selects insertion or deletion. [[T1]] applies the name; [[T4]] cancels. Names can contain up to 23 characters.

Applied edits and names are saved to the working slot without creating a named preset. Holding a sound-slot button creates a separate named snapshot. If SAVE RETRY appears, leave the OP-1 on until it goes away, and don't switch off while it shows. Choosing another drum engine replaces the working kit; save a named snapshot first if you want to keep it. If Gin is removed or switched off, your saved Gin sounds stay on the OP-1 and play again when Gin is back. A damaged kit stays saved but silent; pick a factory kit or another snapshot to fix the slot.

The LFO doesn't change Gin's own sound settings. Only a Tremolo LFO, which moves the volume, and an LFO on the drum effect do anything with Gin.

Dense drum patterns with heavy effects or other processing can still exceed the OP-1's audio budget. The four-voice cap limits Gin's work; it does not guarantee every combination is crackle-free. Watch the resource monitor's peak page and reduce the combined load if needed.
