Tape (echo)

Vintage tape echo.

A tape echo with a moving tape loop on screen. The animation is locked to the audio: a mark takes exactly the delay time to travel from the record head to the play head.

Load[[Shift]] + [[T3]] pick tape on a synth or drum slot

[[cap:knob-b]]
AgeNew to worn

Darker, more saturated repeats

[[cap:knob-g]]
Feedback0 to about 110 %

How much of the echo comes back. Above 100 % it self-oscillates, safely

[[cap:knob-w]]
Time30 ms to 1.45 s

Delay time. It glides like real tape speed, so the pitch bends while you turn

[[cap:knob-o]]
Wow and flutterSubtle in the lower half

Slow pitch drift and fast flutter

Tape has a second page. Push blue, green or white to put that knob on page 2, and push again to go back. blue sets the play heads, one per click: one head, two (halfway and at the end), three (at the thirds), dotted (three quarters) or swing (two thirds). green is the mix. In the middle you hear the dry sound at full level plus the echo, as tape always sounded; turn it down and the echo fades out, turn it up and the dry sound does, until only the echo is left. white locks the delay to the OP-1's tempo. The first push turns sync on at 1/8 and shows the division; turn white for another one: 1/16, 1/8 triplet, dotted 1/16, 1/8, 1/4 triplet, dotted 1/8, 1/4, dotted 1/4 and 1/2 (all the way left is free). Push again for FREE, and the next push brings back the division you had. While it's locked, the white dial turns into a box with the division in it. A division longer than the tape's 1.45 s plays at half length. Older sounds open with one head, the mix in the middle and free time, and play as before.
