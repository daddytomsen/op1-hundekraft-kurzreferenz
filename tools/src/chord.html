Chord mode

Chord mode turns every key into a chord. It knows about scales and progressions, so you can play a whole song with one finger. It works with every synth, including the stock ones, and with every sequencer. It's off until you switch it on.

Open the chord page

  1. Hold [[Shift]] and press [[help]]. You get the clock or an Oblique Strategies card. Either one works.
  2. Press [[T4]]. The chord page opens.
  3. Push orange to switch chords on. Push it again to switch them off.
  4. Press [[T4]] again to close the page. Chord mode stays on until you switch it off.

The scales are major, minor, dorian, mixolydian, lydian, phrygian, locrian, harmonic minor, melodic minor, phrygian dominant, pentatonic, minor pentatonic and blues.

Page B: voicing, strum and drum chords

Hold [[Shift]] and push orange on the chord page to open page B. Do it again, or just push orange, to go back to page A.

EncoderTurnPush
blueVoicing: auto, inv 1, inv 2, inv 3, drop 2 or spreadBass: add the chord's root an octave below
greenStrum: off, or 1/96 to 1/2 beat between notesStrum direction: up, down or random

On page B a star means the bass note, the strum or drum chords are on. Drum chords have no label of their own: the star under Human shows them.

Auto is the normal voicing: voice leading picks the inversion. The other voicings are fixed shapes and ignore voice leading. With a strum set, the first note sounds at once and the others follow one by one. Human and Shape only change a strum, and velocity only makes a difference on sounds that respond to it. With drum chords on, a key on the drums also plays the chord's other notes as pads. When anything on page B is not at its default, page A shows VOICE+. Page B settings are kept after a restart.

Degree mode

Each key plays the chord that belongs to it in the chosen scale. In C major, C plays C major, D plays D minor, G plays G major, and so on. The key you press is always the bass note, so higher keys give higher chords, and the octave keys work as usual.

  • Hold [[Shift]] while you press a key to play a dominant 7th on it. On the fourth degree [[Shift]] swaps minor and major (iv and IV), and on the raised fourth it plays a half-diminished chord.
  • Keys outside the scale play what green push chose. LEAD plays a dominant 7th that leads into the next scale chord, PASS a diminished 7th passing chord, and BORR a borrowed chord (for some keys a slash chord, such as D/F# in major).
  • blue push adds colour to the scale's triads in degree mode: sus4, sus2, the scale's own 7th, or add9. [[Shift]] chords, LEAD and PASS chords and step mode stay as they are.

Step mode

Turn orange to STEP. Now every key press plays the next chord of the progression on white. The key you press only sets the register. Hold [[Shift]] and press a key to step back.

The progressions come in seven groups, in this order:

  • Pop: pop, 50s, sad, royal, royal8, circle, pnk8, 8bar, dreamy.
  • Rock: rock, blues, creep, mario, heyjude, backdr (backdoor), grunge, aeolian, mixo.
  • Jazz: jazz, turn, min251, soul, leaves (Autumn Leaves), rhythm (Rhythm changes), tritone, giant (Giant Steps), neosoul, citypop, ipanema, mblues (minor blues), gospel.
  • Classic: canon, pachel2, fifths, monte, folia, romanes (romanesca), pas.mez (passamezzo antico), pas.mod (passamezzo moderno), mcanon, bergam, ruggiero, neapol (Neapolitan sixth), phryg (Phrygian cadence), picardy, pedal, chopin.
  • Bass: canonb, mcan2, lament, bach, whiter, letdwn, cliche, mcliche, chacon (chaconne), rising.
  • Film: epic, incept, mediant, hero, dark, space, boss.
  • World: andalus, montun (montuno), reggae, highlife, dorian.

Your own "mine" comes last. Hold [[Shift]] and turn white to jump to the first progression of the next or previous group; in step mode the group's name shows under white. Folia, romanes, pas.mez, mcanon, mcan2, mcliche, chacon, lament, aeolian, incept, citypop, mblues, neapol, phryg, picardy, chopin, dark, boss and dorian are minor progressions: they start from a minor chord on the root you set, whatever the scale.

Some progressions have chords with their own bass note: everything in the Bass group, plus gospel, neapol, phryg, pedal and chopin. They show as slash chords: G/B is G major with B in the bass. The chord sounds above its bass. In these progressions each bass note goes as close as it can to the one before, so the bass line walks up and down as written: canon's bass steps down C, B, A, G, F, E instead of jumping up an octave halfway. The key you press sets where the line starts, and the line stays within an octave of it. The name at the top shows the slash chord, and the tab shows the bass as a step of the key after the numeral, so V/7 is the V chord over the 7th. With Bass on (page B), the bass note is doubled an octave lower.

Record your own progression

  1. In degree mode, push white. The page shows "rec".
  2. Play up to 16 chords. Colours and [[Shift]] chords are recorded too.
  3. Push white again. Your progression is saved as "mine".
  4. Turn orange to STEP and white to "mine" to play it back one press at a time.

In degree mode, [[Shift]] + push white clears "mine". It follows the root, so you can transpose it with blue.

Good to know

  • Chords play only in the POLY play mode. In mono, legato and unison each key plays a single note.
  • A triad uses three of the six voices and a 7th uses four, plus one for a slash bass (and one more with Bass on page B). Holding several chords steals voices from each other.
  • USB MIDI out sends every note of the chord, strummed notes at their strum times.
  • With scale mode on, keys are mapped into that scale before chord mode sees them, so out-of-scale chords can't be reached. Switch scale mode off when you use chord mode.
  • Settings and your recorded progression are saved and survive a restart.
