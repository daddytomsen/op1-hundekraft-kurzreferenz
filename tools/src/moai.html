Moai

A Minimoog-style synth with 121 sounds.

Moai by fungiboletus is modelled on the Minimoog Model D: fat basses, singing leads and brassy stabs, with 121 sounds to start from. Every voice has three oscillators and noise, a four-pole ladder filter with emphasis, drive and saturation, and a contour that sweeps the filter. Loudness comes from the OP-1's own envelope on [[T2]], as on the other synths.

Load[[synth]] then [[Shift]] + [[T1]] pick moai

The home screen is the moai of Ahu Tongariki at dusk, and the drawing is filtered the way the sound is. With the cutoff closed you see only the long outlines. Open it and the detail comes in, and with a contour the detail flashes in on each note and fades. The ridges on the left of the horizon are the filter's response, and the island on the right is the contour. Noise blows across the sky as wind. The sound number shows at the top left when it changes, and a knob's name and value show at the top right while you turn it.

EncoderNameDoes[[Shift]] + turn
blueSoundOne of 121 sounds, 000 to 120: 32 oscillator setups and 89 presets, in a fixed shuffled order. It wraps round at both endsDetune
greenCutoffThe filter's cutoffDrive
whiteEmphasisThe filter's resonanceAmount: how far the contour moves the filter
orangeContourOne knob that goes from no contour, through plucks, to slow swellsSaturation

Turning to a number sets the whole sound, so a number always sounds the same. Tweak it from there and save it like any other sound. Start with 000, a bass that sweeps on every note (play it low and short), and 001 to 004, four sounds after Giorgio Moroder's "Chase". The list of all 121 has a name for each number.

Push an encoder for a detailed view. There the four encoders turn the four values named above the dials.

PushView
blueThe whole synth as one panel. [[Shift]] + turn moves the highlight along it. Push blue again to go home
greenThe oscillators, one row each, plus a row for modulation and noise, with a scope of the latest note. Push again for the next row
whiteThe filter. Push again for the contour's attack, decay, sustain and release
orangeThe analog view: detune, drift, drive and saturation

A saved sound keeps seven values: the sound number, cutoff, emphasis, contour, detune, drive and drift. What you change on the other views lasts until you load a sound or pick a new number, so it isn't saved. The LFO moves the four knobs of the view you're on.

Moai is heavy. Six voices use about 60% of the CPU by our measurement, and we aim for 35% for a synth, so watch the CPU meter on big chords. With four or more notes sounding, Moai turns oscillator 3 off on every voice to make room, and the oscillator view says POLY.
