QUANT

Play a part loosely, hear it back in time.

QUANT records what you play and loops it, pulled onto the beat. Pick QUANT with [[Shift]] + [[seq]], then press [[seq]] to open its page.

Hold [[Shift]] and push orange to record. The metronome counts one bar, then QUANT records for the number of bars you set. When they're up, the loop plays straight away. Your keys still play over it.

QUANT keeps every note as you played it. Grid, strength and swing only change where the notes play, so you can turn them back at any time and nothing is lost.

An overdub counts in over the loop's last bar and records one pass on top. Undo takes off the last pass. A loop holds up to 256 notes. Notes keep the length you played, and the end moves with the start. Changing the bars on a recorded loop changes its length; notes past the end stay and come back when you make it longer.

To put the loop on tape, hold [[REC]] and press [[Shift]] + the high C#. QUANT and the tape start together, from the loop's first beat (see Starting in time with the tape). Without [[REC]], those keys start QUANT from the top.

The screen is a piano roll. White bars are the notes where they play, and the grey under them is where you played them. The orange line is the playhead. On the right you see BARS, GRID, STRENGTH and what QUANT is doing: COUNT, REC, DUB, PLAY or STOP. Hold [[Shift]] for SWING and VEL.

Synth and drums each have their own loop and settings, and both are kept after a restart. The octave arrows move a synth loop. With Scale on, the loop keeps the notes Scale gave you. The count-in clicks come from the count-in plugin; without it QUANT still counts the bar, silently.
