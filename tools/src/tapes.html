Four tapes

The OP-1 has four tapes. Each has its own four tracks, clips, loop, position, tempo, album and undo history.

  1. Hold [[tape]] and press [[T1]], [[T2]], [[T3]] or [[T4]].
  2. The tape stops, switches, and a popup shows the tape and its tempo, for example TAPE 2 and 125 BPM.

Tape 1 is the OP-1's own tape, so it's still there if you go back to official firmware. Tapes 2 to 4 are made the first time you open them: empty, at the tempo you're using, and they only take space on the flash as you record. Hold [[tape]] and press the number of the tape you're on to see which one it is. [[T1]] to [[T4]] on their own still pick tracks. Official firmware only opens tape 1 and leaves the other tapes alone.

Note

Switching doesn't work while you record ("stop recording first") or in disk mode. While a clipboard copy runs, a tape switch is refused: wait for the copy to finish, then try again. A new tape needs 64 MB free. Disk mode shows only the tape you're on. There's no way to delete a tape yet.

The OP-1 starts on the tape you used last. It opens on tape 1 and switches to that tape a few seconds later. If the tape is gone or can't be opened, it stays on tape 1. The idea is u/Infamous_Call_6492's.

To copy tracks from tape 2, 3 or 4 to your computer, switch to that tape first, then open disk mode. The disk then holds that tape's tracks and album.

To put a backup back, switch to that tape, open disk mode and copy the files onto it. The restored audio needs that much free flash, and each visit to disk mode uses about 13 MB as well. When you eject, the OP-1 restarts and lists the files it found; press any key to go on.
