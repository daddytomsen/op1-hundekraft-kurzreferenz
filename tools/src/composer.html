Composer

Hold a key, get a whole song.

Pick a style and hold a key: Composer writes a whole song, with verse, chorus and bridge and proper chord changes. There are 43 styles, from deep house and drum & bass to bossa nova, ragtime and film noir, and each brings its own chords, grooves and song form. A song is built from 8-bar sections: A is the verse, B the chorus (the drop in club styles), C the bridge (the break in club styles, funk and nu-disco). Pop runs A A B A B C B B, lo-fi just A A B A. The first time a section comes round it plays as written. After that it changes a little each time, as much as Vary allows. Change the style while it plays and the next bar follows.

Pick Composer in the sequencer browser: hold Shift and press [[seq]], turn blue to composer, then press [[seq]] to load it. With [[seq]] on, hold a key to play the song and let go to stop. Hold Shift while you let go and it keeps playing; [[STOP]] stops it. While it plays, the top line names the section, then a letter for which pass of it this is, then the bar. VERSE·A 3 is the verse as written, in bar 3; CHORUS·B is the chorus the first time it comes back changed. After D it starts again at A. The letters in the top line are the style's song form, one per section, each the first letter of its name in that style: V for verse, C for chorus, D for drop, B for bridge or breakdown. The one playing now is lit red, with a line under it that grows bar by bar. Next to the page names, a triangle means it's playing, and a red ring around it means it's latched. The notes area draws each part in its own shape: the lead as a line, the bass as bars, the arp as a staircase and the chords as stacks. On the drum page Composer plays the drums for the same song, with a groove of its own for every style: see Drums below.

To change the harmony on the fly, hold another key while the song plays. From the next bar the music follows the chord on that key, and it goes back to the song's own chords when you let go. The song's key never moves, so parts you record stay in tune with each other.

The styles, in the order blue goes through them: deep house, techno, trance, synthwave, pop, lo-fi, city pop, ambient, IDM, drum & bass, garage, acid, dub techno, breakbeat, nu-disco, boom bap, trap, neo-soul, afrobeats, amapiano, reggaeton, bossa nova, ballad, blues, gospel, funk, folk, cinematic, chiptune, darksynth, vaporwave, film noir, canon, baroque, classical, romantic, chorale, ragtime, boogie-woogie, nocturne, impressionist, neo-classical and montuno. Three long names are shortened on screen: BOOGIE, IMPRESSION and NEOCLASSIC.

The last six are written for a piano engine. Their parts are called RIGHT and LEFT: record RIGHT on one track and LEFT on another. There's no separate bass or chords part, since LEFT plays both.

With Vary at 0, every song sticks to the style's usual chords and rhythms. Turn it up and new songs wander further: other chords from the style, a borrowed chord now and then, new rhythms built from the style's own. Tension makes the bolder chord moves more likely.

Some styles leave a part out. Boom bap, blues, film noir and chorale have no arp. Canon has no chords, because its arp figure already plays them, and the six piano styles have neither bass nor chords. Turning Role skips a missing part, so you never land on a silent one. Switch to a style that lacks the part you picked and Composer plays the style's next part instead. Your pick comes back with a style that has it.

Push blue to switch between PLAY and SONG. Push green for a new song: new chords, rhythm and melody. Push white for new notes on the same rhythm. Push orange to undo the last change, and again to bring it back. Undo covers the style, song, role and key settings. Changes you make while it plays come in at the next bar. A push that has nothing to do right now is dimmed, such as undo with nothing to undo, or jump while Next shows -.

After a jump in SONG mode, the song form carries on from that section. With LOOP, B ONLY or C ONLY the section plays once and the mode takes over again. Push it while Composer is stopped and the next key starts at that section. Swing and length are saved with your settings, and undo works on them. Lock isn't saved.

To record Composer to tape, it works like the stock sequencers:

  1. Pick the tape track to record on.
  2. Hold [[REC]] and press a key. The tape starts recording and the song starts with it.
  3. Keep the key down, press Shift, then let go of the key. Composer keeps playing.
  4. Let go of Shift and [[REC]]. The tape keeps recording. Press [[STOP]] when the song is done.

Don't press Shift + [[PLAY]] to start the tape: that runs the tape backwards, and a recording goes backwards too.

To build a track, record one role at a time on its own tape track: bass first, then chords, then the lead, with the same style and song. Start every take at the same spot on the tape and press the key on the first beat, so the sections line up.

Drums

Composer writes drums for the same song. In drum mode, pick it the way you did on the synth page: hold Shift, press [[seq]], turn blue to composer and press [[seq]]. Starting and stopping work the same too: hold a key to play, hold Shift as you let go to keep it going, and press [[STOP]] to stop.

The drum page shares the song with the synth page: the style, the sections, swing, length and dynamics. Change the style on one page and the other follows. The drums move with the form. The chorus gets open hats, an extra kick and a crash on its first bar, the bridge drops to a breakdown or half time, and the bar before a new section ends in a fill. While the drums play, hold any other key to mute that key's pad, and let go to bring it back. Holding the kick key gives you a quick drop. The key you started with doesn't mute anything.

Push blue to step through PLAY, SONG and PADS. Push green for a new groove, white for new fills, and orange to undo the last drum change, kit and pad changes included. Fills come in the last bar of a section before it changes, and now and then when a section repeats, so you hear a new fill the next time one comes round. At Fills 0 there are none and the push is dimmed. The Shift page works as on the synth page, with two drum pushes: green gives the kick and snare a new rhythm, white gives the hats and ghost notes a new pattern. Swing and length are shared. Lock and jump only touch the drums.

The OP-1 runs only the sequencer of the page you're on, so the drums and the synth part never play at the same time. Record the drums to a tape track of their own, then switch to the synth page and record the other parts over them, starting each take at the same spot.

Composer expects TE's standard kit layout, which the factory kits use. Counting keys from the left: kick on key 1, snare on 3, rim 5, clap 6, tambourine 7, closed hat 9, open hat 11, low tom 13, ride 14, high tom 15 and crash 16. The toms are a guess, because kits put different sounds on those keys. If your kit is laid out another way, open the PADS page, turn blue to a voice and press the key that has its sound. You hear the key as you press it. Turning green moves the pad one key at a time. Push orange to go back to the stock layout. The pad map is saved with the drum page's settings.

Turn Chord mode off for the lead and bass, or every note becomes a chord. Global Scale leaves Composer's notes alone: they are already in key. The octave keys move every role, so set each part's range with them. Your settings and the current song come back after a restart, with playback stopped.
