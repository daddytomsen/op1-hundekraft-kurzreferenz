DRIFT

A neural net in place of an oscillator.

DRIFT has no oscillator in the usual sense. A small neural net, trained on real instruments and classic synths, draws every waveform while you play, hundreds of times a second, right on the OP-1. The four encoders don't set a filter or an envelope. They move you through what the net learned, so one turn can bend a flute towards a bell, or land somewhere between a choir and a bass that nobody ever recorded. Each note keeps changing while it sounds, and how hard and how high you play changes it too.

Press [[synth]], hold [[Shift]] and press [[T1]], then pick DRIFT in the list.

The encoders don't have one job each. Together they set where you are in the net's sounds, so one turn can change the tone, the attack and the noise in the sound at once. The easy way in is to load a preset and turn one encoder at a time. The pushes do nothing. [[T2]] is the envelope as usual, and an LFO on the knobs moves through the sounds too.

The screen is a landscape made from the sound. The sun's height follows the filter, and the red arc over it the resonance. A glider flies in over each note's pitch and climbs away when you let go. The pink dots are the three presets nearest to where the encoders are, and the four ticks along the top show the encoders in their colours.

The presets are the places the net knows best, and a good place to start exploring: acid, brass, choir, clarinet, clav, ep tine, flute, fm bass, fm growl, glass pad, harp, kalimba, marimba, metal, organ, pluck bass, pluck saw, pwm strings, reso bass, square lead, sub bass, swell pad, sync lead, tom drop, trumpet, tubular bell and violin.
